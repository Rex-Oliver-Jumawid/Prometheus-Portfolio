The homepage presents the floating Furniture Odyssey book directly beneath the hero as a short, book-only second viewport. The antique Prometheus Library shelf follows it and places Furniture Odyssey into the existing furniture-project slot. The existing `#work` navigation target points to this section. `/gallery` remains an isolated preview of the same section. Both server-rendered routes compose `ProjectGallerySection`; its small client boundary owns the interactive scene and reader. Project identity, URLs, summary, and the book repository's six sample pages live in `src/content/projects.ts`.

The Three.js module is imported within 250px of the viewport. The book is fetched with an abort signal, without a shared GLTF cache. The pillar model and poster are no longer requested or displayed. An intersection observer and document visibility listener pause rendering offscreen. A resize observer frames the book on desktop and mobile; pixel ratio is capped at 1.5. The mobile canvas reserves space beneath the book for the title and controls.

The gallery background and typography follow `C:/Users/JAC/Documents/projects/furniture-odyssey-book`: charcoal `#181a1b`, a subtle radial ambient glow, pale text, and muted teal accent. `GalleryBackdrop` renders the glow and updates the homepage's transparent gradient along the panel's top edge, revealing the hero beneath it over up to 280px. This feather shrinks to zero as the gallery reaches the top, restoring a fully opaque pinned section; the standalone gallery stays opaque. The WebM background is no longer rendered or requested. Libre Caslon Display and DM Sans are self-hosted through Fontsource; typography and color tokens live in `src/config/brand.css`. Existing text anchors are retained. The visible interaction hint and pause/resume controls remain removed.

Scroll progress runs from zero when the section's top reaches the viewport bottom to one when it reaches the viewport top. The homepage gallery is intentionally shorter than the hero so the book section hands off to the following bookshelf more quickly. The book grows from 28% to 100% of its 4.4m normalized longest dimension with smoothstep easing; scrolling up reverses growth. Idle motion follows the reference's six-second cycle: `0.055 * sin(phase * PI / 3)` vertical float and a `0.008` radian rock, with no automatic Y turn. Drag smoothing uses damping 16 while held and 5 at rest; manual turns return to rest after 1.1 seconds without input. Reduced motion immediately shows the book at full scale without bobbing or rocking. The render loop runs only while visible and motion is allowed, with single renders for manual controls under reduced motion.

The supplied book artwork/model are preserved. Horizontal dragging turns the closed book around Y; vertical dragging tilts it around Z, clamped to +/-0.5 radians. Selecting it opens the reader. The scene is a focusable DOM control: Enter/Space opens the book, Left/Right turns it, and Up/Down tilts it, without a separate open button or rotation arrows. Loading failure or context loss reveals a large centered book cover. With JavaScript disabled, the section still shows its title, summary, cover, and a prompt to enable the interactive reader.

The reader uses a transparent fullscreen native dialog containing a book, rather than a panel. Its two HTML pages and animated cover share the original 1054:1493 page aspect ratio. A size container fits the largest proportional spread within the available width and height, up to a 1440px spread, without stretching the artwork or pages. Paper edges use a thin 3px stack and a narrow binding shadow. The dialog does not scroll; individual pages retain native scrolling for longer content and small screens, with their scrollbar tracks hidden. The reader contains no repository link, page numbers, or pagination footer. The original cover artwork appears only during opening and closing. Its front face hides as it crosses the edge-on position, so the artwork cannot bleed onto the left page; no cover back-face overlay is rendered. The cover is unmounted throughout reading and page turns, and preloaded at low priority when the gallery is ready.

The scene projects the model's front-cover corners into viewport coordinates (or uses the fallback cover bounds). An affine transform maps the reader cover to that position, projected scale, and angle for the 1.2-second arrival, cover-opening, and page-unfold sequence with paper edges, hardcover shadows, and a shaded binding. Closing reverses the sequence over 0.75 seconds and restores focus to the floating book. The model holds its displayed pose during reading and closing, redraws the same frozen pose behind the closing animation, and resumes idle motion only after the handoff. A model loaded while reading receives the current scroll scale before freezing. The gallery render loop pauses while reading, and background scrolling is locked. Escape and a click outside the book close it; opening focuses the first page, and no close button is displayed. Both pages stay side by side on mobile.

Arrow controls at the left and right center of the book and Left/Right keys turn a sheet over the center binding in 0.7 seconds. The outgoing page remains on the sheet's front and the destination page appears on its back; the underlying spread exposes the correct pages beneath it. The current spread commits only after the turn ends. Controls are locked during a turn, duplicated sheet content is hidden from assistive technology, and the outgoing page's scroll position is preserved. Closing settles an in-progress turn before folding the pages shut. Reduced motion opens, paginates, and closes immediately. Animation-end handlers and cleaned-up fallback timers complete transitions, including when CSS events are unavailable. This CSS reader preserves semantic, selectable HTML text without depending on WebGL or modifying the GLB's cover meshes.

The scene owns and disposes renderer/context, animation frames, resize observer, pointer listeners, environment target, geometries, materials, textures and image bitmaps. The React boundary owns and disposes intersection observers, scroll/resize listeners, media listeners, visibility listener, fetch cancellation. Loading that completes after unmount is disposed instead of attached to the page.

## Book-to-library handoff

`ProjectBookHandoff` is the only scroll/ownership coordinator. It sums section heights in document flow, so sticky visual rectangles cannot alter the timeline. The book lifts as soon as the work section pins, before the incoming shelf can occlude it. Timing, path bow, shadow arrival, and late-load recovery constants live in `src/lib/three/book-handoff.ts`. The source and target register small `view()`/`own()` endpoints when ready; registration also wakes a stopped transition after an asynchronous load.

The two original canvases remain in their hosts. A lazy transparent handoff renderer borrows the live gallery GLTF object for each synchronous draw, then restores its parent and transform before either original scene can render. It renders only the book and lights, with no cover image, border, frame, or traveling background. The gold glow and particles remain CSS effects in the work section. Geometry/material/texture ownership never transfers. GPU-generated environment maps cannot cross WebGL contexts, so the handoff builds its own matching RoomEnvironment PMREM for lighting only. It disposes that environment target, its renderer, cloned lights/shadow maps, context listener, and canvas.

Source bounds and the destination pose come from live object matrices, geometry, camera projections, and measured canvas viewports, including the library's 16:9 letterboxing. There are no authored screen coordinates. Camera-space normalization lets the layer interpolate the orthographic source and perspective shelf projection while preserving both endpoints exactly. Quaternion interpolation rotates the physical book, and a screen-space bow gives the path curvature. Both scenes and the handoff use AgX/exposure consistently; lights blend continuously, including scaled area-light dimensions. A shadow-only shelf caster gradually grounds the book near arrival, without drawing duplicate geometry in the color pass or popping the shelf shadow at ownership transfer.

The coordinator renders and commits exclusive ownership before the browser paints: source at zero, traveling object between zero and one, shelf at exactly one. Reverse scroll follows the same timeline. The layer is disabled outside the work/library corridor and clipped against the actual footer boundary as it enters. Resize remeasures both views. A stopped scroll does not continuously redraw the handoff. The library resets cached GPU bindings before each on-demand draw; without this, repeated identical scene draws could alternate the brightness of baked shelf textures, including across ownership changes. If the shelf is late, the live book holds in the transparent layer, then catches up to the current scroll position when the actual target is ready, without requiring another scroll. Reduced motion performs a direct ownership switch without creating a traveling renderer, and takes precedence if enabled during late-load recovery. WebGL failure preserves the existing readable gallery fallback.

`tests/e2e/book-handoff.spec.ts` exercises ownership, down/up scrolling, pauses, wheel reversal, resize, refresh, delayed loading, and reduced motion, with screenshot attachments at key checkpoints and within-run pixel comparisons across both ownership boundaries. These comparisons cover appearance as well as DOM ownership and catch missing books and lighting discontinuities without machine-specific golden files. The matrix tests verify projected endpoint agreement and borrowed-resource cleanup. The shelf environment remains remotely hosted at the pinned revision in `src/content/library.ts`; offline loading can therefore fall back rather than produce a shelf transition.
